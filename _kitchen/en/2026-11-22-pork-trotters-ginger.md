note: Known locally as *zhu jiao jiang* (猪脚姜) or ginger-vinegar trotters, this traditional Cantonese dish has long been tied to Lingnan family milestones and sharing celebratory bowls with relatives and friends. Alongside home kitchens, the craft also holds local heritage recognition—**the craft of making Nansha pork trotters with ginger** is listed in the seventh batch of district-level intangible cultural heritage in Nansha, Guangzhou ([Official listing](https://www.gzns.gov.cn/gznsqfb/gkmlpt/content/10/10908/post_10908831.html)).

Chopped pork trotters were singed to clear fine bristles, blanched clean, and arranged in the pot with peeled, lightly crushed old ginger before simmering gently with peeled hard-boiled eggs and two bottles of Guangzhou time-honored Jammy Chai sweet glutinous rice vinegar. As the braise reduced over low heat, the skin turned rich mahogany while the ginger and eggs absorbed the deep amber glaze.

These four frames trace the dish from the finished serving bowl back through initial prep, the sweet rice vinegar base, and a closer look at the slow-braised textures.
